# Who Mo Speech is for

Three users today, and one planned.

---

## 1. The instructor

**Who:** the adult who sets Mo Speech up and looks after it. Most often a parent,
but also a grandparent, carer or older sibling. In the app they're the
**instructor**.

**Their situation:** busy, often on the move, frequently holding the tablet in
one hand. Rarely an AAC expert. They want the child communicating, not to
spend evenings configuring software.

**What they need:**

- To get started **in minutes**, with no card and no setup work.
- To **build and personalise** boards quickly: type a list of words, and have
  the pictures found for them.
- To **control what the child sees**, and to trust that it stays that way.
- To use **their family's language**, and their own voice where it matters.
- To **share the work** with another parent, a grandparent or a carer.

**What Mo Speech gives them:** default boards from the first minute,
Auto-match, an instructor view with per-student permissions and a lock,
languages and voices per student, recordings, and invites for family members.

## 2. The student

**Who:** a child (or young person) who uses AAC to communicate. Often autistic.
Many are **gestalt language processors**: they learn language in whole phrases
first, and break them down over time.

**Their situation:** communication happens in the moment, and has to be fast.
They rely on **where** things are as much as what they look like. They may
find motion, clutter or unexpected change distressing. Many are very good at
finding their way around settings.

**What they need:**

- A board that **doesn't move**, so they can learn it by position.
- To say whole things **quickly**: a phrase, a sentence, a request.
- To be heard **in their language**, and with **feeling**.
- **Only** what they need on screen, with more as they grow.
- **Pictures that mean something** to them.

**What Mo Speech gives them:** stable, colour-coded boards; a talker with core
words and phrases one tap away; sentences played block by block or fluently;
tones; their own language and voice; a student view with only what they're
allowed; and personal photos.

## 3. The speech and language therapist (SLT)

**Who:** a professional who assesses and supports AAC users, and often
recommends the tool a family uses. Mo Speech's first pilot is with an SLT in
India.

**Their situation:** they see many families, and need a tool they can
recommend with confidence: one that supports current practice (including GLP)
and the family's language.

**What they need:**

- A tool that supports **phrase-first** learning as well as word-by-word.
- **Modelling**, to show a child where a word lives.
- **Correct vocabulary** in the family's language, and a way to report
  corrections.
- A reason to recommend it that's good for the family, and good for them.

**What Mo Speech gives them:** phrases and block sentences, modelling mode,
content made properly in each language, and (planned, M3) an **affiliate**
arrangement and a way for the team to correct symbol words quickly.

## 4. The school (future)

**Who:** teachers and school staff supporting the same child during the school
day.

**Today:** there's no school product. A teacher can be **invited into a family's
account** (Max plan) and work on the same boards. See the Invites section in
[FEAT-106](../4-builds/features/FEAT-106-settings.md).

**Planned:** a separate school product that connects to the family's, so home
and school stay in step. It's not scheduled before launch. See
[`50-scope.md`](50-scope.md).
